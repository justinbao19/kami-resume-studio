## Design Context

### Users

The primary users are Chinese-speaking job seekers preparing resumes for campus recruiting, experienced-hire applications, and career changes. The product should also support English resumes for overseas applications and ATS-oriented workflows. Their core job is to turn raw career material into a credible, polished, role-specific resume without manually wrestling with layout.

### Brand Personality

Editorial, refined, and trustworthy. The interface should create calm and confidence while users work on a high-stakes document. It should feel like a careful publishing tool with strong taste, not a generic AI dashboard or a novelty template marketplace.

### Aesthetic Direction

Preserve Kami's warm-paper, ink-blue, serif-led editorial identity as the foundation. Use a quiet light interface with paper as the visual protagonist, restrained controls, precise typography, and generous breathing room. The editing experience may borrow the calm and directness of Notion, but the finished resumes must retain a distinctive print-design character. Avoid neon AI gradients, glassmorphism, card-heavy dashboards, decorative charts, and overly playful template thumbnails.

### Design Principles

1. Content first: editing and hierarchy must make career evidence easier to write, scan, and verify.
2. Paper is the product: the resume preview should remain visually dominant and print-accurate.
3. Quiet controls: reveal advanced settings progressively and keep the default workflow focused.
4. Credibility over decoration: templates may vary in personality, but every design must remain recruiter-readable and ATS-conscious.
5. Bilingual by construction: Chinese and English typography, density, labels, and export behavior are first-class concerns.
